<Level n={1} title="The gist" minutes={3} teaser="Every goods movement is also a journal entry. Here's how SAP picks the accounts." soon>

In SAP, logistics and finance aren't separate worlds. Each goods receipt, goods issue, invoice and payment creates an accounting entry automatically, and in S/4HANA all of them land in one table, the **Universal Journal (ACDOCA)**, together with their controlling details such as cost center, profit center and order.

The postings you'll meet most often:

| Business event | Journal entry |
|---|---|
| Goods receipt for a purchase order | Dr Inventory / Cr GR/IR clearing |
| Supplier invoice | Dr GR/IR clearing / Cr Supplier (payables) |
| Payment run | Dr Supplier / Cr Bank |
| Components issued to production | Dr Production order / Cr Raw material inventory |
| Post goods issue to a customer | Dr Cost of goods sold / Cr Inventory |
| Customer billing | Dr Customer (receivables) / Cr Revenue, plus tax |
| Customer payment | Dr Bank / Cr Customer |

How does SAP know which G/L accounts to use? **Account determination.** For materials management it's configured in OBYC: the material's **valuation class** combined with a transaction key, such as BSX for inventory, WRX for GR/IR and PRD for price differences, points to the account. For sales, VKOA finds the revenue accounts. If it's set up wrongly, quantities are right but the financial statements are wrong, a mistake that's hard to spot.

**Price control** matters too. With **moving average price (V)**, the stock value updates with each receipt; with **standard price (S)**, stock is valued at a fixed price and differences go to a price difference account.

**Controlling (CO)** adds the management view: cost centers, profit centers, internal orders, product costing and margin analysis. Month-end **close** brings it together: open the next periods, clear GR/IR, post accruals, revalue foreign currency, run depreciation, settle production orders, then produce the statements. The close is only as clean as the logistics postings feeding it.

<FromPractice>

My material CHSP1077 used valuation class **3100**, which pointed its goods receipts to the trading goods inventory account. Two real lessons from the exercises: a typo of 2,500 instead of 2,550 on an invoice created a price difference aimed at the inventory account (the material uses moving average price), and in Round 2 the GR/IR account carried a **510 USD** difference, because 100 units were invoiced but only 80 received, until the last 20 arrived.

</FromPractice>

<SayIt>

In SAP every logistics event posts automatically to finance: goods receipt debits inventory and credits GR/IR, the invoice clears GR/IR against the supplier, goods issue posts cost of goods sold, and billing posts the receivable and revenue. Account determination, OBYC for MM using the valuation class and keys like BSX, WRX and PRD, and VKOA for SD revenue, chooses the accounts, and in S/4HANA everything lands in the Universal Journal, ACDOCA. Month-end close then clears GR/IR, settles orders, runs depreciation and revaluation, and closes periods.

</SayIt>

<Q q="How does SAP decide which inventory account a goods receipt posts to?">

Through automatic account determination in OBYC: the material master's valuation class, combined with the transaction key BSX for inventory postings (and the chart of accounts and valuation grouping code), points to the inventory G/L account. The offsetting GR/IR posting uses key WRX.

</Q>

<Q q="What is the difference between standard price and moving average price?">

Standard price (S) values stock at a fixed price; any difference between that and the actual purchase or production cost goes to a price difference account. Moving average price (V) recalculates the stock value with every receipt at its actual price, so differences flow into the stock value while stock is available.

</Q>

<Q q="Why do old GR/IR balances worry auditors?">

They mean goods were received but never invoiced, or invoiced but never received. That can signal missing liabilities, duplicate or wrong invoices, or unposted receipts, so they're reviewed and cleared at period end.

</Q>
