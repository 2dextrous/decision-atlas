<Level n={1} title="The gist" minutes={3} teaser="Every goods movement is also a journal entry. Here's how SAP picks the accounts." next="How it works" nextMinutes={3}>

In SAP, logistics and finance aren't separate worlds. Each goods receipt, goods issue, invoice and payment creates an accounting entry automatically, and in S/4HANA all of them land in one table, the **Universal Journal (ACDOCA)**, together with their controlling details such as cost center, profit center and order.

The postings you'll meet most often:

| Business event | Journal entry |
|---|---|
| Goods receipt for a purchase order | Dr Inventory / Cr GR/IR clearing |
| Supplier invoice | Dr GR/IR clearing / Cr Supplier (payables) |
| Payment run | Dr Supplier / Cr Bank |
| Components issued to production | Dr Consumption, charged to the production order / Cr Raw material inventory |
| Post goods issue to a customer | Dr Cost of goods sold / Cr Inventory |
| Customer billing | Dr Customer (receivables) / Cr Revenue, plus tax |
| Customer payment | Dr Bank / Cr Customer |

How does SAP know which G/L accounts to use? **Account determination.** For materials management it's configured in OBYC: the material's **valuation class** combined with a transaction key, such as BSX for inventory, WRX for GR/IR and PRD for price differences, points to the account. For sales, VKOA finds the revenue accounts. If it's set up wrongly, quantities are right but the financial statements are wrong, a mistake that's hard to spot.

**Price control** matters too. With **moving average price (V)**, the stock value updates with each receipt; with **standard price (S)**, stock is valued at a fixed price and differences go to a price difference account.

**Controlling (CO)** adds the management view: cost centers, profit centers, internal orders, product costing and margin analysis. Month-end **close** brings it together: open the next periods, clear GR/IR, post accruals, revalue foreign currency, run depreciation, settle production orders, then produce the statements. The close is only as clean as the logistics postings feeding it.

<FromPractice>

My material CHSP1077 used valuation class **3100**, which pointed its goods receipts to the trading goods inventory account. <KeyIdea>

Every logistics event posts automatically to finance. Account determination (OBYC for MM, VKOA for SD) picks the accounts from the valuation class and transaction keys, everything lands in ACDOCA, and month-end close depends on clean logistics postings.

</KeyIdea>

<Level n={2} title="How it works" minutes={3} teaser="OBYC keys and valuation classes in more detail, moving average price recalculated by hand, the Global Bike typo explained, and the month-end close in transactions." next="Deep dive" nextMinutes={3}>

### Account determination in more detail

OBYC combines the **chart of accounts**, a **valuation grouping code** (to treat plants differently if needed), the material's **valuation class** and a **transaction key** to find the account. Key transaction keys:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Key | Used for |
|---|---|
| BSX | Inventory postings (every movement that changes stock value) |
| WRX | GR/IR clearing |
| PRD | Price differences (mainly with standard price) |
| GBB | The offsetting entry for inventory postings, refined by modifiers such as VBR (consumption), VAX/VAY (goods issue for sales), INV (inventory differences) and BSA (initial stock entry) |
| UMB | Revaluation gains or losses |

</div>

Standard valuation classes include 3000 for raw materials, 3100 for trading goods, 7900 for semi-finished and 7920 for finished products, each pointing to its own inventory account.

### Moving average price, worked

<Example title="a new receipt changes the average">

Stock: 100 units valued at 25.00 each (2,500). A goods receipt brings 300 units at the PO price of 25.50 (7,650).

New moving average price = (2,500 + 7,650) / 400 = 25.375.

</Example>

When a later invoice differs from the PO price and the material uses moving average price, the difference is posted to the inventory account (BSX) and the average updated, as long as enough stock remains to absorb it. That is what happened with the Global Bike typo: an invoice entered at 2,500 instead of 2,550 created a difference aimed at the inventory account. With standard price, the same difference would have gone to the price difference account (PRD), leaving the stock value unchanged.

### The close, in transactions

- **Periods:** open the new posting period in FI (OB52) and in materials management (MMPV).
- **GR/IR:** analyse and clear old items, then reclassify balances for the balance sheet.
- **Foreign currency:** revalue open items and balances (FAGL_FCV).
- **Fixed assets:** run depreciation (AFAB).
- **Production:** calculate work in process and variances, and settle orders (CO88).
- **Reporting:** produce the financial statements and management reports.

<KeyIdea>

OBYC uses valuation class and transaction keys (BSX, WRX, PRD, GBB with modifiers, UMB) to find accounts. Moving average price updates with each receipt and absorbs invoice differences while stock lasts, and the close runs through periods, GR/IR, revaluation, depreciation, settlement and reporting.

</KeyIdea>

### Controlling in S/4HANA

- **Cost center accounting:** costs of departments and functions.
- **Internal orders:** costs of projects, campaigns or events.
- **Product costing:** standard costs, actual order costs and variances.
- **Margin analysis:** profitability by customer, product and region. In S/4HANA the account-based approach (margin analysis) is the standard, and because postings land in ACDOCA, finance and controlling report from the same line items.

### The material ledger

The material ledger is always active for inventory valuation in S/4HANA, recording values in several currencies or valuations. Companies can also use **actual costing**, which collects price and exchange-rate differences during the period and revalues inventory and cost of goods sold to actual cost at period end.

### Valuing stock at the lower of cost

Accounting standards require inventory at the lower of cost and net realisable value. SAP supports this with balance sheet valuation functions that compare stock values with market prices, movement rates or range of coverage, and propose write-downs for slow-moving or overvalued stock, which links the slow and obsolete stock stop to the books.

### "Posting only possible in periods…"

A goods receipt or goods issue fails with a message that posting is only possible in two specific periods when the materials management period hasn't been moved forward (MMPV), even though FI's period is open. Materials management allows postings only in the current and previous period, so the period roll-over at month start is a small step with large consequences.

### What makes a close fast

- Clean logistics postings during the month: receipts on time, invoices matched, orders settled.
- Automated reconciliations: GR/IR, intercompany, subledgers to the general ledger.
- A close calendar with owners and dependencies, monitored daily.
- Exceptions investigated during the month, not discovered at its end.

<Trap>

Assuming finance can fix logistics errors at month end creates a slow, painful close. Every wrong price, missing receipt or unsettled order becomes a manual correction later; the cleaner the daily postings, the faster the close.

</Trap>

<KeyIdea>

Controlling and finance share ACDOCA in S/4HANA, the material ledger is always on, balance sheet valuation applies lower-of-cost rules, the MM period roll-over (MMPV) prevents classic posting errors, and a fast close depends on clean daily logistics postings.

</KeyIdea>

<Q q="How does SAP decide which inventory account a goods receipt posts to?">

Through automatic account determination in OBYC: the material master's valuation class, combined with the transaction key BSX for inventory postings (and the chart of accounts and valuation grouping code), points to the inventory G/L account. The offsetting GR/IR posting uses key WRX.

</Q>

<Q q="What is the difference between standard price and moving average price?">

Standard price (S) values stock at a fixed price; any difference between that and the actual purchase or production cost goes to a price difference account. Moving average price (V) recalculates the stock value with every receipt at its actual price, so differences flow into the stock value while stock is available.

</Q>

<Q q="Why do old GR/IR balances worry auditors?">

They mean goods were received but never invoiced, or invoiced but never received. That can signal missing liabilities, duplicate or wrong invoices, or unposted receipts, so they're reviewed and cleared at period end.

</Q>
