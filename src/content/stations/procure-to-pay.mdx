<Level n={1} title="The gist" minutes={3} teaser="Six steps, the T-codes behind them, and the accounting each one triggers." next="How it works" nextMinutes={3}>

Procure to pay in SAP follows the business process step by step, with a transaction (and a Fiori app) for each:

| Step | T-code | What happens | Finance posting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase requisition | ME51N | Internal request, often created by MRP | None |
| RFQ and quotations (optional) | ME41, ME47, ME49 | Invite bids, enter quotes, compare prices | None |
| Purchase order | ME21N | Binding order to the supplier | None (a commitment) |
| Goods receipt | MIGO, movement type 101 | Stock increases | Dr Inventory / Cr GR/IR clearing |
| Invoice verification | MIRO | Three-way match against PO and goods receipt | Dr GR/IR / Cr Supplier |
| Payment | F110 | Payment run pays due, unblocked invoices | Dr Supplier / Cr Bank |

The **GR/IR clearing account** is the bridge: the goods receipt credits it and the invoice debits it. If they don't balance, something was received but not invoiced, or invoiced but not received, and old GR/IR balances are a classic audit question.

At invoice verification, SAP compares price, quantity and dates against **tolerances** (set in OMR6). An invoice outside tolerance is **blocked for payment**, and someone must release it (MRBR) once the cause is fixed. The payment run (F110) skips blocked invoices.

Master data does a lot of quiet work: purchasing **info records** remember supplier prices, **source lists** restrict allowed suppliers, and the material master's valuation class decides which inventory account a goods receipt hits.

<FromPractice>

I ran this cycle twice for material CHSP1077. **Round 1:** requisition `10066332` for 300 units; an RFQ (`7000000204`) compared Boomtown at 26.00 with Space Bike at 25.50, and Space Bike won; PO `4500000546` for 300 × 25.50 = 7,650 USD; goods receipt `5000000985`. Invoice `5105600427` was blocked by the date tolerance because it arrived 31 days early; after release it was paid in payment `1500000157`. That PO also created info record `5300000521`, so in Round 2 the source was found automatically.

**Round 2:** PO `4500000554` for 100 units, but only 80 arrived at first (`5000000993`). Invoice `5105600428` for 100 was blocked for quantity, and GR/IR showed a 510 USD difference. Receiving the last 20 (`5000000994`) removed the cause, yet the invoice stayed blocked until I released it; it was paid in `1500000158`.

</KeyIdea>

### What shapes a purchase order

- **Document type:** NB for a standard purchase order, UB for a stock transport order between plants.
- **Item category:** standard, or special ones such as K (consignment), L (subcontracting), S (third-party) and D (service).
- **Account assignment:** stock items need none; items for consumption carry one, such as K for a cost center, F for an order or A for an asset, so costs go to the right place.
- **Release strategy:** approval rules by value, purchasing group or other criteria. Requisitions and orders can be released individually (ME29N for orders) or collectively (ME28).
- **Sources:** info records supply prices and conditions, source lists restrict the allowed suppliers, and contracts or scheduling agreements set long-term terms.

### Tolerances and blocks

Tolerance keys in OMR6 set limits for each kind of variance at invoice verification, such as **PP** (price), **DQ** (quantity) and **DW** (date). An invoice outside a limit posts but is **blocked for payment**. The payment run (F110) skips it until someone releases it with MRBR, either after fixing the cause or after deciding the variance is acceptable. In Global Bike, Round 1's invoice was blocked by the date tolerance and Round 2's by quantity.

Goods can also arrive into **blocked stock** (movement type 103) or quality inspection stock and be released later (105), so receiving and accepting are separate steps when needed.

<KeyIdea>

Document type, item category, account assignment and release strategy shape each PO. Receipts and invoices meet in GR/IR through keys BSX, WRX and PRD, and tolerance keys such as PP, DQ and DW decide which invoices are blocked until released.

</KeyIdea>

### Special procurement processes

- **Consignment:** the supplier's stock sits in your warehouse; you own it only when you withdraw it. Liabilities are settled periodically from withdrawals (MRKO).
- **Subcontracting:** you send components to a supplier who processes them and returns a finished item. Components move to stock provided to the vendor (movement type 541) and are consumed when the finished item is received.
- **Third-party (drop-ship):** the supplier ships directly to your customer; often triggered from a sales order.
- **Stock transport orders:** move stock between your own plants, in one step or two (goods issue into transit, then goods receipt), optionally with deliveries and shipping documents.

### Outline agreements

- **Contracts** (ME31K) fix prices or values over a period; release orders draw against them.
- **Scheduling agreements** (ME31L) set delivery schedules for repetitive supply, common in automotive and process industries.

With source lists, info records and agreements in place, approved requisitions can be converted to purchase orders automatically (ME59N).

### Evaluated receipt settlement

With **ERS**, the buyer doesn't wait for a supplier invoice: the system creates the invoice from the goods receipt and the agreed price (MRRL). It removes most invoice mismatches and speeds up payment, but it needs accurate prices in the system and suppliers who agree to it.

### Keeping GR/IR clean

At period end, GR/IR balances are analysed and old items investigated: goods received but never invoiced, invoices for goods never received, small differences that can't be traced. Small, explained differences can be cleared with MR11, and balances are reclassified for the balance sheet. Old, unexplained balances are a classic audit finding, so most companies review them monthly.

### Common P2P problems

- A requisition isn't converted: no source of supply, or the source list blocks the supplier.
- A goods receipt fails: no open quantity, the PO isn't released, or the posting period is closed.
- An invoice is blocked: variances beyond tolerance, or GR-based invoice verification with the receipt missing.
- Wrong prices: outdated info records or conditions copied into new orders.
- Duplicate invoices: the duplicate-invoice check depends on reference numbers being entered consistently.

<Trap>

Releasing blocked invoices in bulk to "clear the queue" defeats the three-way match. Each block points to a mismatch in price, quantity or date; fix the cause or document why the variance is accepted.

</Trap>

<KeyIdea>

Know the special procurement processes (consignment, subcontracting, third-party, stock transport), use outline agreements and automatic PO creation, consider ERS where prices are reliable, review GR/IR monthly, and treat each blocked invoice as a signal, not an obstacle.

</KeyIdea>

<Q q="What is the difference between MIRO and F110?">

MIRO records and verifies the supplier invoice against the PO and goods receipt and creates the payable. F110 is the automatic payment program that pays due, unblocked payables in a batch and generates the payment file. MIRO creates the liability; F110 settles it.

</Q>

<Q q="What accounting entries does a goods receipt against a PO create?">

For a stock material: debit inventory (via account determination key BSX) and credit the GR/IR clearing account (WRX). The invoice later debits GR/IR and credits the supplier; price differences go to inventory or a price difference account depending on stock and price control.

</Q>

<Q q="Why was an invoice blocked even though the goods arrived?">

Something exceeded tolerance: price above the PO price, quantity invoiced above quantity received, or a date variance such as invoicing far earlier than planned. Even after the cause is fixed, the payment block stays until the invoice is released.

</Q>
