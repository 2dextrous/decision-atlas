Some supply chain rules aren't about cost at all; they're the law. They change how products are stored, moved, labelled and traced, and breaking them can mean recalls, fines or a shut warehouse. A few common families:

- **Food safety:** hygiene, temperature control, shelf-life management and traceability. In India these are set under FSSAI regulations.
- **Pharmaceuticals:** good distribution practice (GDP) covers temperature-controlled storage and transport (the cold chain), qualified facilities, documented processes and the ability to trace and recall batches. Many export markets also require serialization, a unique code on each pack.
- **Labelling and legal metrology:** packaged goods must declare things like net quantity, maximum retail price and manufacturing date in a set format, under India's packaged-commodities rules.
- **Dangerous goods:** chemicals, batteries and aerosols need special packaging, labels, documents and trained handlers to store and ship.
- **Producer responsibility:** collection and recycling obligations for plastic packaging and electronic waste.

The supply chain lesson is to **build compliance into the process**, not inspect it at the end. That means first-expiry-first-out picking for short-shelf-life goods, temperature loggers on cold-chain shipments, warehouse systems that record batch numbers at every move, and label templates checked before printing. The payoff is a fast, precise **recall**: when a batch is found faulty, you can find every pack that contains it within hours instead of pulling everything off the shelves.

<KeyIdea>

Regulations shape how products are stored, moved, labelled and traced. Build them into systems and processes, especially batch traceability, so compliance is automatic and recalls are precise.

</KeyIdea>

<Q q="Why is batch traceability so important?">

Because when a defect or contamination is found, you need to know exactly which units are affected and where they went. Good traceability means a targeted recall of one batch within hours; poor traceability means pulling far more product, at much higher cost and risk to customers.

</Q>

<Q q="How would you make a warehouse cold-chain compliant for medicines?">

Qualify the storage areas and equipment, monitor and log temperature continuously with alarms, use validated packaging and temperature-controlled transport, train staff, document procedures and deviations, and track batches through every movement, as good distribution practice requires.

</Q>
