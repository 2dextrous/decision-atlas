<Level n={1} title="The gist" minutes={3} teaser="Your risk sits two or three tiers down, where you have the least visibility." soon>

Most companies know their direct suppliers well. Far fewer know who supplies those suppliers, and that's often where disruptions start. In 2021 the global chip shortage halted car assembly lines around the world, though carmakers rarely buy chips directly: the chips sat two or three tiers down, inside modules made by their suppliers.

**N-tier visibility** means mapping the network beyond tier 1:

1. **Map:** for critical parts, ask tier 1 suppliers where their key inputs come from, down to raw materials if needed.
2. **Find concentration:** several suppliers depending on one sub-supplier, one region or one port is a hidden single point of failure.
3. **Score risk:** financial health, geographic exposure (floods, earthquakes, conflict), quality history, compliance and ESG, and how long the part would take to replace.
4. **Monitor:** news, weather and financial alerts that flag trouble early.
5. **Act:** for the riskiest items, add safety stock, qualify alternatives, redesign to standard parts, or sign capacity agreements.

A useful way to prioritize is to ask, for each part: if this supplier stopped tomorrow, **how long could we keep running** on the stock we have, and **how long would it take to recover** with another source? Parts where recovery takes longer than survival need attention first.

Mapping is hard work. Suppliers may not want to reveal their sources, and networks change constantly, so companies focus on critical items rather than trying to map everything.

<Q q="How would you start building n-tier visibility for a manufacturer?">

Start with the most critical parts, those with high revenue at risk, long lead times or single sources. Ask their tier 1 suppliers to disclose key sub-suppliers and locations, combine that with external data, and look for shared dependencies. Then extend gradually; trying to map everything at once usually stalls.

</Q>

<Q q="Why did many car makers lose production in the 2021 chip shortage even though their direct suppliers were reliable?">

Because the constraint was deeper in the network, with semiconductor makers and their capacity, two or three tiers down. Without visibility of that tier, companies couldn't see the shortage coming, secure capacity early or redesign around it.

</Q>
