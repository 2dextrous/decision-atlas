In the SAP world, demand planning usually happens in **SAP IBP (Integrated Business Planning)**, a cloud planning tool, while execution happens in **S/4HANA**. The flow mirrors the Demand line on this map:

1. **History in:** sales history and customer orders flow from S/4HANA into IBP.
2. **Clean the data:** pre-processing steps correct outliers and fill gaps, so a one-off bulk order doesn't distort the baseline.
3. **Statistical forecast:** IBP offers forecast models such as moving averages, simple, double and triple exponential smoothing (Holt-Winters), automated exponential smoothing, Croston's method for intermittent items, regression, ARIMA and gradient-boosted trees. Planners assign models by segment, or let the system choose.
4. **Measure the error:** IBP calculates error measures, such as MAPE and others, so planners can compare models and track accuracy.
5. **Consensus:** sales and marketing add their input in planning views, often in Excel, and the S&OP process agrees one demand plan.
6. **Short-term sensing:** demand sensing uses recent orders and other signals to adjust the next few weeks.
7. **Hand-off to execution:** the agreed forecast goes back to S/4HANA as **planned independent requirements (PIRs)**, the demand that MRP plans against (in S/4HANA they can also be maintained with transaction MD61).

From there, MRP takes over: it nets the PIRs against stock and open orders and creates planned orders and purchase requisitions.

The concepts don't change because the tool is SAP. Classification, method choice, bias and value-add all still apply; IBP just runs them at scale.

<Q q="How does a forecast in IBP reach production planning in S/4HANA?">

The released consensus forecast is transferred to S/4HANA as planned independent requirements. MRP nets them against stock and open supply, explodes BOMs, and creates planned orders for production and purchase requisitions for bought materials.

</Q>

<Q q="Which IBP forecast model would you use for spare parts with sporadic demand?">

Croston's method, which is designed for intermittent demand, rather than standard exponential smoothing. I'd also check accuracy with measures that handle zero demand, and size stock from the demand distribution.

</Q>
