<Level n={1} title="The gist" minutes={3} teaser="A quick feasibility test against the few resources that really limit output." soon>

A plan can look fine on paper and still be impossible on the shop floor. **Rough-cut capacity planning (RCCP)** is the quick check that catches this early. It doesn't look at every machine; it looks at the few **critical resources**, the bottleneck lines, scarce skills or a key supplier, and asks whether the plan fits.

Say the plan calls for 50,000 units next month, and every unit passes through a filling line that can make 40,000. RCCP shows a 10,000-unit gap before anything is released. Management then chooses how to close it:

- overtime or an extra shift,
- moving some volume to another plant or a co-packer,
- building stock in an earlier month with spare capacity,
- changing the plan itself, by delaying, reducing or shaping demand.

The important nuance: **RCCP finds the gap; it doesn't solve it.** Choosing the cheapest fix is a separate planning or optimization decision.

RCCP has a more detailed cousin. **Capacity requirements planning (CRP)** runs after MRP and checks the load on every work centre, using the actual planned orders and routings. RCCP is fast and approximate, used on the MPS; CRP is detailed and used closer to execution.

<KeyIdea>

Check the plan against the bottleneck before committing. RCCP flags infeasible plans early, while there's still time to add shifts, move volume or change the plan.

</KeyIdea>

If a plan needs 50,000 units and the bottleneck can do 40,000, RCCP flags the 10,000-unit gap, and then planning decides how to close it through overtime, other plants, co-packers, pre-building stock or changing the plan. <Q q="A company plans 50,000 units but its bottleneck line can only make 40,000. What concept flags this, and what can it do?">

Rough-cut capacity planning flags the 10,000-unit gap. Options include overtime or extra shifts, moving volume to another plant or a co-manufacturer, producing earlier and holding stock, investing in capacity, or adjusting the plan. The choice depends on cost, lead time and service impact.

</Q>

<Q q="How is RCCP different from CRP?">

RCCP is an approximate check of the MPS against a few critical resources, done early. CRP is a detailed check of every work centre using MRP's planned orders and routings, done closer to execution.

</Q>
