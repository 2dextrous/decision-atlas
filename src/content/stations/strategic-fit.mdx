<Level n={1} title="The gist" minutes={3} teaser="The supply chain should be good at what the customer is actually paying for." soon>

A company's competitive strategy says which customers it serves and what it promises them: lowest price, widest choice, fastest delivery, newest designs. **Strategic fit** means the supply chain is built to deliver that promise, and not something else.

Two examples:

- A discount retailer competes on low prices for everyday staples. It needs an **efficient** supply chain: full trucks, few large warehouses, high utilization, lean stock.
- A fashion brand launches new designs every few weeks and can't predict which will sell. It needs a **responsive** supply chain: flexible capacity, short lead times, small first orders and fast reorders of what works.

Swap them and both fail. The fashion brand with an efficient, slow supply chain is left with mountains of unsold designs and empty shelves for the hits. The discount retailer with an expensive, responsive supply chain can't keep prices low.

Two tools help check the fit:

1. **Implied demand uncertainty:** how uncertain is the demand the supply chain must serve, given the customer's needs? Short lead times, wide variety, new products and high service requirements all raise it.
2. **Fisher's framework:** functional products (predictable, low margin) belong in efficient chains; innovative products (unpredictable, higher margin) belong in responsive ones.

Fit isn't permanent. As a product matures, demand becomes more predictable and the right supply chain moves from responsive toward efficient.

<KeyIdea>

Strategic fit means the supply chain excels at what customers pay for: efficiency where demand is predictable and price matters, responsiveness where demand is uncertain and speed or novelty matters.

</KeyIdea>

<Q q="Why would a responsive supply chain be wrong for a discount retailer?">

Its customers pay for low prices on predictable staples. Responsiveness means buffer capacity, faster transport and more inventory near customers, all costs the retailer can't pass on. An efficient chain delivers what its customers value.

</Q>

<Q q="How does strategic fit change over a product's life?">

At launch, demand is uncertain, so responsiveness matters most: small batches, flexible capacity, fast reorders. As the product matures and demand stabilizes, the focus shifts to efficiency: bigger batches, cheaper modes, leaner stock. At end of life, the focus is avoiding obsolete stock.

</Q>
